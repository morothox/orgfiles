* Party Overview: Elephant and Donkey (Worksheet)

** Tasks
  *Task 1:* List the main differences between the two parties.
  - *Economy & Taxes:*
     
    - Republicans: Favor free-market capitalism, deregulation, and private spending over state spending. Oppose higher corporate taxes and state intervention.
     
    - Democrats: Favor higher taxes on the rich/corporations and reduced taxes for the middle and lower classes to reduce the deficit and fund public services.

  - *Welfare & Social Programs:*
    - Republicans: Skeptical of large government welfare systems; prefer targeted grants and private initiatives to prevent abuse.
    - Democrats: Believe the government must support welfare, healthcare, and education to assist disadvantaged groups and guarantee basic care for everyone.
  - *Environmental Policy:*
    - Republicans: Argue that strict environmental regulations hurt economic growth and kill jobs; prioritize business interests.
    - Democrats: Prioritize environmental protection and clean energy; oppose tax breaks and subsidies for oil corporations.
  - *Foreign & Defense Policy:*
    - Republicans: Advocate a strong military, high defense spending, and unilateralism (the right of the US to act alone in its national self-interest).
    - Democrats: Rely on diplomacy and multilateralism (acting through international alliances like NATO and the UN); aim to reduce military spending.
  - *Demographics & Geography:*
    - Republicans: Mostly white, Protestant, married with children, often without a college degree. Strongest in rural areas, suburbs, the South, and the West.
    - Democrats: Broad coalition of minorities (African Americans, Hispanics), Catholics, Jews, labor unions, and young/educated voters. Strongest in large cities, the Northeast, and the Pacific Coast.

  *Task 2:* Find corresponding parties in Germany and compare the parties' programs.
  - *Republican Party $\leftrightarrow$ CDU/CSU & FDP:*
    - /Similarities:/ Like Republicans, the CDU/CSU stands for conservative values, law and order, and national security. The FDP mirrors the Republican belief in free markets, private enterprise, and skepticism towards state regulations.
    - /Differences:/ The US Republicans are positioned much further to the right than the German CDU/CSU. In Germany, even conservatives support universal healthcare, strict gun laws, and a strong social safety net, which Republicans often reject as "socialism".
  - *Democratic Party $\leftrightarrow$ SPD & Bündnis 90/Die Grünen:*
    - /Similarities:/ The Democrats' focus on social justice, workers' rights, and a strong welfare state corresponds with the SPD. Their commitment to climate protection, renewable energy, and multilateral foreign policy is very close to the Green Party.
    - /Differences:/ The Democrats are a broad big-tent party. Many moderate Democrats would be considered centrist or even slightly conservative in Germany, and the party remains strictly capitalist, without the traditional European democratic-socialist roots of the SPD.

  *Task 3:* Choose an issue from the excerpt you are interested in and find out more details.
  - *Chosen Issue: Environmental Policy & Energy*
    - /Democratic Position:/
      - Climate change is treated as an urgent crisis that requires decisive federal regulation.
      - Support heavy government investment in renewable energy (solar, wind) and green infrastructure (e.g. the *Inflation Reduction Act*).
      - Rejoin international climate treaties (Paris Agreement) and enforce strict emissions standards while cutting tax incentives for fossil fuel companies.

* Political Scandals: Donald Trump (Hush Money Affair)

** Overview & Key Facts:
- *The Incident:* In October 2016, shortly before the presidential election, Trump's personal lawyer Michael Cohen paid $130,000 in hush money to adult film actress Stormy Daniels to keep her from going public about an alleged sexual encounter with Trump in 2006.
- *The Cover-Up:* Trump reimbursed Cohen in monthly installments throughout 2017. In the Trump Organization's internal records, these payments were falsely booked as regular legal expenses based on a non-existent retainer agreement.
- *Legal & Political Consequences:*
  - Michael Cohen pleaded guilty in 2018 to campaign finance violations and tax evasion, serving prison time.
  - In April 2023, Manhattan District Attorney Alvin Bragg indicted Trump on 34 felony counts of falsifying business records to influence the 2016 election.
  - In May 2024, a New York jury found Trump guilty on all 34 felony counts, making him the first former US president to be convicted of a felony.

** Worksheet Question: How damaging are political scandals for a leader?
- *Comparison & Impact:*
  - *Richard Nixon (Watergate):* Completely fatal. Nixon lost bipartisan support in Congress and resigned in 1974 to avoid impeachment and conviction.
  - *Bill Clinton (Lewinsky Scandal):* Moderately damaging. Clinton was impeached for perjury and obstruction of justice in 1998, but the Senate acquitted him. The strong economy and public perception of it as a private matter allowed him to maintain high approval ratings.
  - *Donald Trump (Hush Money / Scandals):* Polarized impact. Unlike Nixon, Trump kept his core support. His base viewed the indictments as a politically motivated "witch hunt" by Democrats, which energized his voters and drove campaign donations. However, it hurt his standing among moderate and independent suburban voters.
  - *Conclusion:* A scandal's damage depends heavily on party polarization, congressional support, and how effectively the leader frames the investigation.

* Assignments: Death of a Salesman

** Assignment 05.03.26 (Reading until p. 65)

- *How does Linda criticize her sons?*
  - Linda confronts Biff and Happy about their neglect, lack of respect, and lack of care for their father.
  - She criticizes Biff for being ungrateful and distant: he doesn't write letters, never settles down, and wanders from job to job.
  - She calls out Happy for prioritizing shallow fun, women, and partying over supporting his parents.
  - She fiercely defends Willy ("Attention, attention must be finally paid to such a person"): he is not a great man, but an exhausted human being who worked his entire life for them.
  - She reveals Willy's actual situation: he lost his salary, works on commission only, borrows $50 a week from Charley, and is suicidal (the recent car crashes were deliberate and she found a rubber hose on the gas pipe in the basement).

- *In which situations does Willy escape from reality?*
  - Willy escapes into daydreams and flashbacks whenever reality shatters his illusions:
    - *Exhaustion and driving trouble:* When he can no longer drive safely or make sales on the road, he retreats into memories of young Biff as a high school football hero.
    - *Financial problems:* When Linda presents the long list of unpaid bills, proving he earned much less than he claimed, he shifts into fantasies where clients love and admire him.
    - *Guilt over the affair:* Seeing Linda mend her stockings triggers memories of giving new stockings to his mistress in Boston. Overwhelmed by guilt, he yells at Linda and escapes into memories of family unity.
    - *Despair and failure:* When he feels helpless, he hallucinates conversations with his dead brother Ben, who became wealthy in the African diamond mines, asking him for the secret to effortless success.

** Assignment 06.03.26 (DS) (Reading until p. 74 & p. 90)

- *Which plan do the boys have? Why do they want to do that? (until p. 74)*
  - *The Plan:* The "Florida idea" / sporting goods business. Biff and Happy plan to form a joint company ("The Loman Brothers") selling sporting equipment. Biff wants to ask his former boss, Bill Oliver, for a $10,000 loan (which Willy raises to $15,000 in his mind).
  - *Why they want to do it:*
    - Neither brother wants to take orders from bosses they dislike; they want independence.
    - Biff loves outdoor, physical work, while Happy wants business prestige and status.
    - The plan gives the family immediate hope and temporarily restores Willy's pride in Biff.

- *Steps leading to Willy’s suicide (until p. 90):*
  - *Fired by Howard:* Willy asks his young boss Howard for a New York desk job with a modest salary ($40/week). Howard is dismissive, only interested in his wire recorder, and fires Willy on the spot when Willy brings up his past loyalty. Willy loses his livelihood and professional identity.
  - *Charley’s office / Rejection of help:* Willy goes to Charley to borrow money for his life insurance premium. Charley offers him a real job ($50/week), but Willy's stubborn pride prevents him from accepting it.
  - *Valuing life vs. death:* Willy admits to Charley: "You end up worth more dead than alive." He starts viewing his life insurance policy as the only valuable thing he has left to give.

** Assignment 12.03.26 (Reading until p. 131, l. 9)

- *Further steps leading to Willy’s suicide:*
  - *The bar disaster:* Biff and Happy abandon their father at Frank's Chop House while he is having a breakdown in the bathroom.
  - *Biff’s confrontation:* At home, Biff confronts Willy with reality, breaking down in tears and shouting that both of them are ordinary people living a lie ("I am a dime a dozen, and so are you!").
  - *Willy’s realization:* Willy is deeply moved that Biff actually loves him ("Biff—he likes me! He cried for me!").
  - *The suicide decision:* Willy decides to crash his car so that Biff will inherit the $20,000 life insurance money, believing this will finally make Biff a successful businessman.

- *Why is Bernard important for the story?*
  - Bernard serves as a foil (contrast) to Biff.
  - In high school, Bernard was the quiet, studious boy whom Willy mocked as "liked, but not well-liked" compared to the popular Biff.
  - As an adult, Bernard becomes a successful lawyer arguing a case before the Supreme Court without bragging, proving that hard work and modesty lead to real success, not just charisma.
  - Bernard also reveals the turning point: Biff burned his sneakers and gave up on summer school right after visiting Willy in Boston, confronting Willy with his guilt about the affair.

- *What about Biff’s meeting with Bill Oliver?*
  - The meeting is a total failure: Biff waits six hours, and Oliver does not even remember him.
  - Biff realizes he was never a salesman for Oliver, just a shipping clerk, exposing the family's habit of lying to themselves.
  - In panic and confusion, Biff steals Oliver's fountain pen and runs down eleven flights of stairs, shattering his own illusions.

- *What happens in the bar? (Frank’s Chop House)*
  - Happy and Biff meet at the restaurant; Happy flirts with women and lies about Biff's football career.
  - Biff confesses that Oliver rejected him and that he stole the pen, wanting to be honest with Willy.
  - When Willy arrives, he refuses to listen to any bad news and demands positive results.
  - Willy suffers a breakdown at the table, hallucinating the Boston hotel room and the affair.
  - Biff leaves in frustration, and Happy denies knowing Willy ("No, that's not my father. He's just a guy"), leaving their father alone in the washroom to chase after women.
